About

Gingee (also known as Senji or ) is a historic town in the Viluppuram district of Tamil Nadu, India, world-famous for its massive, structurally impregnable Gingee Fort. Dubbed the "Troy of the East" by the British and ranked as the "most impregnable fortress in India" by Maratha king Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, the fort complex is a listed UNESCO World Heritage Site under the Maratha Military Landscapes of India


Places

Major Forts and Hills

Rajagiri (Raja Fort)

The main and highest citadel (about 800 feet high) featuring a deep natural chasm bridged by stone, a granary, a prison cell, and a 1,500+ step trekking trail.

Rajagiri Fort is the primary and most prominent citadel of the historic Gingee Fort complex located in the Villupuram district of Tamil Nadu.Originally built around 1200 AD, it stands majestically on a rocky hill at an elevation of about 800 feet.Maratha ruler Chhatrapati Shivaji famously ranked it as the "most impregnable fortress in India," while the British designated it the "Troy of the East".The fort features a deep natural chasm at its summit that could only be crossed via a strategic wooden drawbridge.The inner complex houses brilliant architectural structures managed by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), including the multi-story Kalyana Mahal, ancient temples, granaries, and a sacred water reservoir.

Krishnagiri (Rani Fort)

The smaller northern hill fort, requiring a milder climb of about 500–600 steps to reach old temples and watchtowers with panoramic views.
